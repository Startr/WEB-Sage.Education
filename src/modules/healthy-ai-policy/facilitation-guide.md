**Purpose:** The four parts are written self-paced, but they land hardest with a leadership team in the room. This companion gives the facilitator (principal, IT lead, coach) session timing, setup, and plays for the hard moments. Read it before Part 1. For the 3-hour express lane, use the companion `mvp_track.md` instead of the timing table below.

---

## Suggested timing (adapt to your team)

| Part | Solo/self-paced | Facilitated team session |
|------|-----------------|--------------------------|
| Part 1 — Grounding the Vision | ~90 min reading + listing | 2 hours (questions 30 · map activity 20 · domains passages + lists 30 · vision draft 30) |
| Part 2 — School-Wide Policy Draft | ~90 min | 2 hours (principles + regulation 20 · template draft 60 · §§2.4–2.6 decisions 40) |
| Part 3 — Continuum & Integrity | ~60 min | 90 min (levels + examples 30 · plagiarism + disclosure 30 · scenarios 30) |
| Part 4 — Classroom Co-Creation | ~45 min | 60 min leaders-only, plus Step 0 staff session (60–90 min) |
| **Total** | ~5 hours Solo | ~7 hours across 3–4 sessions |

Run Parts 1–2 together if time is short; never compress the domains passages-and-lists or scenario discussions — they are where the policy gets its honesty.

## Setup and materials

- Print: the Domain passages (§1.5), the six-level definitions (§3.1), the tool-review checklist (§2.4), the incident contact sheet (§2.6).
- List sheets — paper or a shared digital whiteboard for remote teams — for the Activity 1-B risks-and-hopes lists; chart paper for clustering anxieties (Step 0) and vision drafts.
- Offer anonymity where honesty is risky: anonymous slips for the 1-C emotional audit and Step 0 anxieties, not round-robin confession.
- One shared doc for the three records teams must carry forward: working definitions (§1.2), axis naming (Activity 1-A), vision statement (§1.6).

---

## The hard-conversation playbook

- **The banner ("just ban it").** Don't debate the ban — run the domains. Ask: *which risks would a ban remove, and which hopes would it cost?* Let Activity 1-B do the persuading.
- **The enthusiast ("AI everywhere, now").** Same move, other direction: *show me which three hopes justify maximal use — and what your three risks say back.* A risks list the team wrote itself persuades where arguments don't.
- **The anxious teacher ("I'll look incompetent").** Normalise with Step 0: everyone tastes the levels as a learner first, anonymously if needed. Revisit the 1-C emotional-audit answers together; assign the worried a buddy for their first co-created unit.
- **The lawyer ("what if we're liable?").** Park liability in Part 2's board-alignment box ("If you sit under a board or district") and the Tier 1/2/3 split (§2.6): most fears dissolve once incidents have named owners and a contact sheet.
- **The rusher ("can we finish today?").** Name the cost: a policy written in one sitting is a policy revisited in crisis. Protect the scenario hour (§3.6) and the monitoring decision (template §6 — review cadence plus signals) — they are the cheapest insurance in the module.

---

## Sequence notes (order matters)

1. Activity 1-A (name your axis) before 1-B (risks & hopes) — teams need shared language first.
2. Domain 6 is preview-only; positions on the six levels happen in Part 3.
3. Step 0 (staff learning) before Steps 1–6 (classroom co-creation) — teachers feel the continuum before they teach it.
4. Monitoring signals (template §6 — review cadence plus signals) are decided during Part 2, not "later" — later never comes.

---

## After the last session

Collect the artefacts (definitions record, axis record, vision, risks-and-hopes lists, policy draft, contact sheet) into one shared folder. Set the review date and owner before anyone leaves the room — an undated policy is an unkept one.

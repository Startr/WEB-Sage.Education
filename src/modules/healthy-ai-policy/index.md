### What you'll learn

- How to ground a school's AI use in a shared vision, asking clarifying questions before writing any rules — and distinguishing healthy from unhealthy AI use (beneficial vs. harmful cognitive offloading).
- How to build lessons and units with a **deliberate, balanced mix** of AI use across the AI Integration Continuum — **No-AI → AI-Resistant → AI-Optional → AI-Required → AI-Collaborative → AI-Building** — and how to define AI plagiarism and teach disclosure/citation.
- How to articulate a **school-wide** AI policy first — covering approved tools, incident response, and review — and then, after a hands-on staff session, co-create an **aligned classroom-level** policy with students, all guided by a single mindset axis: **maker (Slow AI) over consumer (Fast AI)**, expressible through four framings.
- How to write a policy that **communicates** — clearly and without jargon — the decisions teachers make about **how, when, and why** AI appears in their classroom.

---

### About this module

This is a **coaching module**, not a fact sheet. It guides school IT leads and building administrators — in K–12 schools of any size, in Canada, the USA, and Europe — to **write their own** AI policy rather than having one prescribed. The module is self-paced and asynchronous, with no fixed time limit, and can be worked through alone or by a leadership team.

The philosophical backbone is the **maker mindset (Slow AI)** — learners (students and teachers) stay **the authors of their own thinking**, using AI as a partner and material — over the **consumer mindset (Fast AI)**, where AI becomes an answer-vending machine. This is articulated through a single axis with **four framings**: maker/consumer, slow/fast, conscious/reflexive, and user/author.

The module is grounded in established frameworks and evidence: ISTE Standards, OECD guidance (adaptive frameworks, not rigid bans), the EC–OECD AILit Framework (Engage / Create / Manage / Shape), cognitive-offloading research, and the emotional/psychological research on AI use in schools. Operational sections draw on COPPA/FTC guidance, US ED assistive-technology and breach-response resources, CDT, and CoSN.

**How this module relates to the AI Healthy Use Framework.** Parts 1–2 write the rules; Parts 3–4 live them — the continuum, integrity, and classroom work below put the policy into lessons. The canonical definitions (continuum levels, AI plagiarism, disclosure standard) live here; the companion Classroom AI Framework teaches them in daily classroom routines without redefining them.

### Who should join?

**Target audience:** School IT leads and building-level principals/assistant principals who will create or revise their school's AI policy for students, teachers, and parents. May also be used by curriculum directors or district teams.

**Prerequisites:** None. No technical expertise in AI is assumed. The module works for a school starting from scratch and for a school revising a confusing or draft policy.

### The AI Integration Continuum (core model)

| Level | Definition (school's own wording) | Example |
|-------|----------------------------------|---------|
| **No-AI** | The work does not involve AI by design; skill and thinking stand alone | First draft, reflection, observation |
| **AI-Resistant** | Designed so the work cannot be completed by AI alone | In-class oral defence, hand-drawn artefact, live demonstration |
| **AI-Optional** | Students may choose whether to use AI, within teacher-set boundaries, and explain the choice — including choosing none | Research task where students decide (and justify) their use or non-use of AI support |
| **AI-Required** | Students use AI, then critique, extend, or build on its output; submission includes AI's contribution + student's judgment | AI draft + student critique |
| **AI-Collaborative** | AI is a working partner; the student decides when to lean on AI and when to set it aside, and explains that decision | Managed research task with two explained decisions |
| **AI-Building** | Students design, build, test, and refine their own AI (chatbot/agent) on an approved, privacy-first platform; the AI is the work | Study-buddy chatbot built, tested with classmates, improved, and reflected on |

**Design principle:** A healthy unit mixes levels deliberately by learning goal — balance is the goal, not maximal AI use.

---

### The mindset axis (one axis, four framings)

| Framing | Healthy | Unhealthy |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| Maker vs. Consumer | Author; AI is material | Audience; submits AI output |
| Slow AI vs. Fast AI (Beghetto & Glăveanu) | Learner in charge of ideas; AI as partner | Rushes to outsource thinking |
| Conscious vs. Reflexive | Deliberate, aware choice | Automatic, unexamined use |
| User vs. Author | Owns the thinking and the final work | Consumes ready-made thinking |

### Learning Objectives

1. **Articulate** working definitions of AI and generative AI by asking clarifying questions and distinguishing their uses, benefits, and risks.
2. **Distinguish** healthy from unhealthy AI use — including beneficial vs. harmful cognitive offloading — and **weigh** benefits/deficits across the benefit/deficit domains (plus the integration preview), to set your school's direction.
3. **Differentiate** the guiding philosophy through the maker/consumer mindset axis (with Slow/Fast, Conscious/Reflexive, and User/Author framings) in language for students, teachers, and parents.
4. **Develop** a school-wide AI policy draft that is clear, plain-language, audience-specific, and aligned to international frameworks and local regulation — covering approved tools, incident response, and review arrangements.
5. **Apply** the AI Integration Continuum to design balanced lessons/units, while **defining** AI plagiarism and **specifying** when, why, and how AI use is disclosed and cited.
6. **Prepare** staff with a hands-on session and **facilitate** co-creation of a classroom-level AI policy aligned to the school-wide policy, and **communicate** AI expectations to parents.
7. **Reflect** on the school's specific context using a question bank to finalize a policy that fits the school.

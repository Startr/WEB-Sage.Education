## Learning Objectives

**Course:** Designing Your School's Healthy AI Policy — A Coaching Module for School Administration and IT Leads
**Audience:** IT leads + building-level principals and assistant principals (K–12)
**Level:** Administrator / Leadership professional learning
**Duration:** Self-paced, asynchronous (no fixed time limit); structured as four sequential parts

### Objectives

1. **Articulate** the school's working definitions of AI and generative AI (GAI) by asking clarifying questions and distinguishing their distinct uses, benefits, and risks for teaching, learning, and administration.

2. **Distinguish** healthy from unhealthy AI use — including beneficial versus harmful cognitive offloading — and **weigh** the benefits and deficits (cognitive, psychological/emotional, social, integrity, equity, and integration) across the benefit/deficit domains (plus the integration preview), to set your school's direction.

3. **Differentiate** the school's guiding philosophy of AI use by adopting a single axis — maker mindset (Slow AI) over consumer mindset (Fast AI) — expressed through four framings (maker/consumer, slow/fast, conscious/reflexive, user/author), in language appropriate to students, teachers, and parents.

4. **Develop** a school-wide AI policy draft that is clear, plain-language, and addresses students, teachers, and parents — covering approved tools, incident response, and review arrangements — grounded in the school's shared vision and aligned with international frameworks (ISTE, OECD, UNESCO) and local/regional (Canadian, US, EU) regulations.

5. **Apply** the AI Integration Continuum (No-AI, AI-Resistant, AI-Optional, AI-Required, AI-Collaborative, AI-Building) to design lessons and units with a planned, balanced mix of AI use, while **defining** AI plagiarism and **specifying** when, why, and how students and teachers disclose and cite AI use.

6. **Prepare** staff with a hands-on continuum session and **facilitate** the co-creation of a classroom-level AI policy (teacher + students) that is explicitly aligned to the school-wide policy, and **communicate** AI expectations to parents in clear, approachable language.

7. **Reflect** on the school's particular context (size, grade bands, values, infrastructure, regulatory environment, and stakeholder needs) using a bank of reflective questions to finalize a policy that fits the school rather than being prescribed.
